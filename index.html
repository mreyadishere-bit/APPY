01

A shelf, not a search box

Diaries are organised the way you actually think about them. Folders hold books. Books hold pages. A book can be a year, a trip, a difficult month, or the week you moved house.

Nothing is buried in an endless feed, and nothing is sorted by a machine that knows better than you.

02

It stays on your device

Your entries are saved on the device you write them on, inside the folders and books you made. The diary is yours in the plain sense: it lives where you do.

Write on the bus. Write with the wifi off. The page is already there.

03

Read it like it was printed

The same diary opens on the website as a book you can turn through — covers that open, pages that move. Reading back what you wrote should feel like reading, not like scrolling through your own archive.

A diary should be a place you return to, not a file you maintain.
